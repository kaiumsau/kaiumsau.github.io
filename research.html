Research areas

We develop analytical methods for contaminants in food and the environment, then use them to answer practical questions: what is in the water, soil, crops and fish of Bangladesh, and how much of it reaches people?

  • Pesticide residues

    Insecticides, fungicides, herbicides and growth regulators in rivers, ponds, fish, fruits, vegetables and cereals; their bioaccumulation in aquatic food chains and dietary risk to consumers.

  • Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons

    The 16 priority PAHs in water, rice and fish, measured by QuEChERS extraction and UHPLC with fluorescence detection.

  • Bisphenol A and plastic-related chemicals

    Bisphenol A in water, juice, sediment, food and fish, tracing how plastics release endocrine-disrupting chemicals into what we eat and drink.

  • Aflatoxins and food contaminants

    Aflatoxins in rice and wheat by UHPLC–fluorescence with post-column derivatization, and screening tools for illegal additives such as potassium bromate in bread.

  • Veterinary drug residues

    Fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines and sulfonamides in water, meat, eggs, milk, feed and fish, which bear directly on antimicrobial resistance.

  • Trace metals and potentially toxic elements

    Lead, cadmium, chromium, copper and other elements in paddy soil, rice, fertilizers and agrochemicals, including areas affected by coal mining.

  • Exposure and risk assessment

    Turning measured concentrations into estimated daily intakes, hazard quotients and cancer risk, so results can inform regulators and the public.

  • Low-cost water treatment

    Simple adsorbent filters for removing organic micropollutants from drinking water in homes and communities.

SAU Food Safety Laboratory

Our analytical work is based in the Food Safety Laboratory at Sher-e-Bangla Agricultural University, which Dr. Kaium leads as officer-in-charge.

Sample preparation
