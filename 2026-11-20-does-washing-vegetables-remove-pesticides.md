---
title: Does washing vegetables remove pesticide residues?
category: Popular science
summary: Washing helps, but how much depends on the pesticide, the vegetable and how you wash it. A short guide for home kitchens.
---

Almost everyone washes vegetables before cooking. But does washing actually remove pesticides, or just dust?

## Where residues sit

Pesticides sprayed on crops end up in two places. Some stay on the **surface** of leaves and fruit skins. Others are **systemic**: the plant absorbs them and carries them inside its tissues. Washing can reach only the first kind.

## What helps at home

- **Rinse under running water** while rubbing the surface with your hands. Running water works better than dipping vegetables in a bowl of still water.
- **Soak for a few minutes**, then rinse again. Studies have found that a weak baking soda solution removes more of some surface residues than plain water.
- **Peel** where it makes sense, such as potatoes, cucumbers and gourds. Peeling removes most surface residues, though some nutrients go with the peel.
- **Remove outer leaves** of cabbage and similar vegetables.
- **Cooking**, especially boiling and discarding the water, breaks down or removes part of many residues.

## What washing cannot do

Washing does not remove systemic pesticides inside the plant, and it does not fix residues caused by spraying too close to harvest. The real solution is on the farm: using approved pesticides at the right dose and respecting the **pre-harvest interval**, the waiting period between the last spray and harvest.

## The bottom line

Wash, peel and cook sensibly, and keep eating plenty of vegetables. Their health benefits are large and well established. At the same time, safer pesticide use by growers and regular residue monitoring are what truly protect consumers.
